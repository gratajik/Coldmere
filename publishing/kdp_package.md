## 2. Categories (3 required)

KDP allows three categories. Here are the browse paths a reader would navigate, chosen to balance relevance against beatable competition.

**1. `Books > Literature & Fiction > Genre Fiction > Horror`**
- **Why it fits:** This is the literary-horror home shelf. The novella is explicitly literary gothic horror, and this category signals the quieter, prose-forward end of the genre rather than splatter or creature-feature.
- **Competitiveness:** High overall, but the novella length and literary framing differentiate it from the dominant full-length supernatural-thriller titles. Realistic top-50 in subcategory, top-5 in a tighter slice.

**2. `Books > Mystery, Thriller & Suspense > Thrillers & Suspense > Supernatural`**
- **Why it fits:** The book is structured as an investigation. Iris decodes the ledgers, follows the evidence, and races a clock. Supernatural-suspense readers who want dread plus a puzzle are the core audience.
- **Competitiveness:** Medium-high. Crowded with longer series, which works in a standalone novella's favor for readers seeking a single complete sitting. Reachable bestseller flag here.

**3. `Books > Literature & Fiction > Genre Fiction > Ghost`**
- **Why it fits:** Narrowest and most precisely targeted of the three. This is a haunted-house story in the truest sense, and the Ghost subcategory has a smaller, hungrier, more loyal readership.
- **Competitiveness:** Lowest of the three and the best bet for an actual #1 New Release / category bestseller badge. Prioritize this one for launch visibility.

> Note: KDP now lets you request additional categories via support after publishing. Once live, consider asking for `Literature & Fiction > Gothic` and `Short Reads` placements if available in your marketplace.

## 4. Pricing

### Ebook

| Stage | Price | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Launch (first 2 weeks)** | **$2.99** | Lowest price that still earns 70% royalty. Maximizes early velocity, reviews, and category-badge momentum for a debut-positioned novella. |
| **Regular** | **$3.99** | Sustainable novella price. Stays in the 70% band ($2.99-$9.99), signals "complete short work, premium prose," and leaves headroom for $0.99 promo spikes later. |

A 19,000-word novella should not be priced like a 90,000-word novel. $3.99 is the credibility sweet spot: high enough to avoid the "throwaway" perception, low enough to convert impulse horror readers.

### Paperback

Novellas are short for print, so fixed printing costs dominate. Assume ~120-140 pages at standard formatting.

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Trim size | 5" x 8" (standard fiction novella trim) |
| Est. page count | ~130 pages |
| Est. KDP print cost (B&W, US) | ~$2.40 |
| **List price** | **$8.99** |
| Approx. royalty (60% list − print cost) | ~$2.99/copy |

$8.99 is the floor that keeps a thin paperback from looking overpriced per page while still clearing a real margin. Going below ~$7.99 risks negative or negligible royalty after print cost.

### Hardcover

KDP hardcover (case laminate) carries a higher fixed cost and suits collectors and gift buyers.

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Trim size | 5.5" x 8.5" |
| Est. page count | ~130 pages |
| Est. KDP print cost (B&W, case laminate) | ~$6.50 |
| **List price** | **$16.99** |
| Approx. royalty (60% list − print cost) | ~$3.69/copy |

Hardcover is a prestige/gift SKU, not a volume driver. Price it for the readers who want a beautiful object of a literary horror novella; the margin per unit is healthy even at low volume.

### Kindle Unlimited (KDP Select)

**Recommendation: Enroll in KDP Select / Kindle Unlimited. Strongly yes.**

- **Why:** Novellas are KU's ideal product. KU readers devour short, atmospheric horror in single sittings, and page-reads (KENP) on a fast, gripping novella convert far better than à-la-carte sales for an unknown author.
- **Trade-off:** 90-day Amazon exclusivity. For a debut-positioned literary horror novella with no existing wide audience, the discoverability and KENP income outweigh wide distribution.
- **Tactics:** Use the 5 free Kindle Countdown / Free Book Promotion days per 90-day term to run a launch-week free or $0.99 push, feeding the Ghost-category badge and review acquisition.
- **Reassess at:** 90 days. If you build a direct newsletter list and want Kobo/Apple/B&N reach, you can go wide on the next book and keep this one in KU as a funnel.

## 6. Comp Titles

Positioning against quiet, literary, character-driven haunted-house fiction (not splatter or paranormal romance). These comps belong in marketing copy and pitch materials, **not** in the keyword field.

| Comp Title | Why it positions well |
|---|---|
| **The Haunting of Hill House** by Shirley Jackson | The gold standard for ambiguous, psychological, prose-forward haunting where the house works on a fragile mind. Sets reader expectation for dread over gore and an unreliable interior POV. |
| **The Little Stranger** by Sarah Waters | Slow-burn class-and-decay gothic, a haunting tied to a single house and its history, with a narrator whose rationality is the point of failure. Mirrors Iris's "I believe in provenance" stance. |
| **Mexican Gothic** by Silvia Moreno-Garcia | Modern commercial-literary gothic crossover that proved a large appetite for atmospheric haunted-house horror with a sharp female lead. Signals contemporary readership, not just classic-lit. |
| **The Grip of It** by Jac Jemc | A house that warps space and logic, dread accumulated through small wrongnesses and a couple's unraveling. Closest match for the "room larger inside than out" and the creeping-architecture horror. |
| **Wakenhyrst** by Michelle Paver | Fenland gothic, old ledgers and obsessive record-keeping, a buried family secret and a marshy, drowning landscape. Strong tonal twin for Coldmere, Fen Lane, and the mill-pond. |

**"For fans of" variations (for the description and ad copy):**

- *For fans of the patient dread of Shirley Jackson and the fenland gothic of Michelle Paver.*
- *For readers who loved the slow architectural horror of Jac Jemc and the class-haunted decay of Sarah Waters.*
- *In the tradition of Mexican Gothic and The Little Stranger: a haunted house, a sharp and unreliable narrator, and a secret a hundred years deep.*
- *If The Haunting of Hill House and Wakenhyrst kept you up at night, Coldmere is waiting at the end of Fen Lane.*

## 7. Review Strategy

### ARC Distribution Plan

**Timeline: begin 4 to 6 weeks before launch.** A novella is fast to read, so reviewers can turn it around quickly, but you still want reviews staged to land *on* launch day, not trickling in afterward.

| Channel | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct ARC list (newsletter)** | 30-50 readers | Your warmest reviewers. Send EPUB + a short, no-pressure ask for an honest review on launch week. Highest conversion. |
| **BookSirens / BookFunnel** | 50-100 downloads | Genre-targeted ARC distribution to horror/gothic readers. BookSirens skews toward readers who actually post reviews. |
| **NetGalley (co-op or self-listed)** | Optional | Higher cost; better for review *volume* and for catching librarians/bloggers. Consider only if budget allows; a novella can succeed without it. |
| **Goodreads pre-launch** | Add the book early | Create the listing, run a giveaway if budget permits, and seed early "want to read" shelvings. |
| **StoryOrigin** | Newsletter swaps + ARC team | Build a recurring ARC team here for this book and the next. |

**The ask (keep it short and compliant):** request an *honest* review, never a positive one. Disclose nothing on the reader's behalf; Amazon wants reviewers to mark ARCs as such. Do not gate, trade, or pay for reviews.

### Blogger / BookTuber / BookTok Outreach Targets

Target by *niche fit*, not follower count. For a literary gothic novella, a 2,000-follower horror-specific account outperforms a 200,000-follower general book account.

**Outreach categories to pursue:**

- **Horror-specific Booktubers and bloggers** who cover literary and atmospheric horror (the kind who review Jackson, Waters, and quiet haunted-house fiction, not just slasher and creature features).
- **#GothicTok / #HorrorTok creators** who do mood-driven, quote-card, and "books that unsettled me" content. The novella's "room that's warm" and "something counting in the walls" hooks are tailor-made for 15-30 second video.
- **Bookstagram gothic/horror aesthetic accounts** that pair atmospheric flat-lays with short reviews. Offer them a physical paperback or hardcover for photogenic content.
- **Novella-and-short-fiction reviewers** specifically. A subset of reviewers prizes short, complete works and will champion a tight novella precisely because it respects their time.
- **Indie / self-published horror community accounts and roundup blogs** that actively look for debut and small-press horror to feature.

**Pitch hook to lead with:** "A literary haunted-house novella narrated entirely in the language of an estate inventory, until the narrator becomes an item in it. Quiet, patient, no gore. Readable in one sitting." That single sentence does most of the qualifying for you.

**Key timing principles:**

1. **Stagger so reviews land at launch.** Get ARCs out 4-6 weeks early; ask reviewers to hold or post on launch week so the page opens with social proof, not a blank review section.
2. **Front-load the discount.** Run the $0.99 or free KU promo in launch week, when review velocity and category-badge momentum compound. The **Ghost** subcategory is your most attainable #1 New Release flag; aim the promo at it.
3. **Honest reviews only, always.** Never trade, gate, or pay for reviews, and never imply a positive one is expected. Amazon will strip reviews and can penalize the listing.
4. **Nudge once, then stop.** A single polite follow-up to non-responders two weeks post-launch is fine. More than that costs goodwill.
5. **Update the description after launch.** Once you have a few strong reviews or any editorial quote, add a pull-quote to the top of the HTML product description. (There is room; the current draft uses under half the 4,000-character limit.)
