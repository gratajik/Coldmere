PROPOSED: Late reveals re-derive comprehension the reader already owns; the mechanic gets re-explained at each subsequent "reveal," landing the same understanding 2-3 times
LAYER: 6
PLACEMENT: after §6.11 Cross-Chapter Callbacks
EVIDENCE: The reader pass flagged this as the #1 reader-experience issue in all 5 passes — consistently the single biggest fix in every verdict. The pattern: an engine/mechanism the reader fully understood by a mid-book chapter gets re-walked ("the warm room runs warm when a name is fresh... the count climbs to the lot") before each later beat delivers its genuinely-new fact, so the new fact lands in "already-cleared air."
EXISTING COVERAGE: §6.11 covers callbacks needing visceral recognition, and §6.1 covers telling-after-showing concentrating near endings. Neither covers the distinct failure of *re-deriving an already-established explanation* before each subsequent reveal in a chain of escalating reveals. This is about reveal economy across a sequence, not a single explanatory sentence or a single callback.
EXACT TEXT:

**Once a mechanism is understood, later reveals must deliver only the NEW fact — not re-derive the engine.** When a story has a chain of escalating reveals built on one underlying mechanism (a rule, a system, a pattern the reader is meant to decode), the model re-explains the whole mechanism at the top of each subsequent reveal before delivering that reveal's new information. The reader, who understood the mechanism several chapters ago, experiences the genuinely-new fact landing in already-cleared air — the same comprehension delivered two or three times. Each later reveal should open directly from its new concrete datum and state the consequence ONCE, trusting the reader to carry the mechanism forward. Detection: for every reveal after the one that first establishes a mechanism, check whether its opening re-walks logic the reader already owns (the tell is a paragraph that re-states "X happens because Y" where X→Y was decoded earlier). Fix: cut the re-derivation paragraphs; lead with the undisclosed fact (the written name, the new object, the changed evidence) and let the established mechanism stay implicit. The strongest beat of the chapter should arrive at the start, not after a recap.
