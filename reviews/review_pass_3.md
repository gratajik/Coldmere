## B. Publisher & Reviewer Panel

### 1. Acquisitions Editor

This is a genuinely commercial literary gothic with a clean high-concept engine: an estate-clearance archivist must empty the house of the dead aunt who, it emerges, *fed her own brother to the house thirty years ago*. The catalog-frame conceit — a woman whose profession is to inventory the dead becoming an item in someone else's inventory — is a strong, sellable hook, and the bureaucratic-horror register ("Lot eight" in the wall instead of a moan) is distinctive enough to stand out in a crowded haunted-house field. Comp titles: *The Grip of It* (Jac Jemc) for the architectural wrongness and prose discipline; *The Haunting of Hill House* for the keeper/house possession; *Mexican Gothic* and *The Spite House* for the commercial-literary gothic shelf; *Piranesi* for the impossible-interior measurements and the kept-by-the-house cosmology. At ~19K words it's a novella, which positions it for a single-volume genre imprint, a novella line (Tor.com-style), or a literary-horror anthology lead.

The manuscript is acquirable *now* on concept and execution of its best chapters (3, 7, 14, 20, 23, 24 are publication-strong). What stands between it and a clean acquisition is not story — the architecture is sound and the reveals are well-sequenced — but a **systemic voice-control failure that did not get fixed across two prior revision rounds** (M1, M2, M3 below). The uncontracted-narration problem and the two over-budget signature-phrase families are exactly the patterns a discerning reader registers as "AI-smooth" or "over-written," and they are concentrated, ironically, in a book whose whole aesthetic is *precision*. The coda's voice-convergence (M5) is the single most commercially damaging craft issue: the trap only closes if Marian reads as a different person, and right now she reads as Iris. Fix the voice pass (which is mechanical and bounded) and the C5 grounding gap, and this is ready.

### 2. Developmental Editor

Structurally this is in strong shape and most of my prior developmental concerns have landed. The setup/payoff ledger is honored on the page: the unsquared count (ch1) → impossible measurement (ch15); the century-deep register (ch13) → Danny's dated entry (ch14) → Iris's own name (ch21); the dial-tone lag (ch2/10) → the Danny-thing's speech lag (ch20). The anti-"chosen one" discipline holds — Iris deduces every move from evidence the reader watched her gather, and the coda's trap (burning the book doesn't stop the keeping) is reconstructable from ch22's keeper-succession material. The wrong-belief framing at the climax (M13) and the grounded-cold door (M14) both landed cleanly — these were my biggest prior worries and they're resolved.

My remaining developmental notes are about **redundant arrival** in the back third. Ch16 already lands "I am the last entry, not yet called" with full force; ch21 and ch22 then re-derive the same conclusion (M11), and ch19 re-runs ch11's window-watch architecture (M12). The back third has three chapters (16, 21, 22) arriving at one destination and two chapters (11, 19) executing one architectural move. The *artifacts* differ — ch16 is deduction, ch21 is the literal dated entry, ch22 is Edith's complicity — but the prose re-explains the shared mechanism each time, flattening the escalation curve the emotional-arc table wants (knowing-she's-next → terror-at-her-name → pity-for-Edith). The fixes are structural trims, not rewrites: stop re-teaching the warm-room mechanism in ch21, lean ch19 into the forensic "it has been placed for me" register to distinguish it from ch11's "is it real," and let ch22 end on pity rather than restating the fact for a third time. These are surgical structural cuts that will tighten the descent without touching the architecture.

### 3. Copy Editor

The prose-level habits are this manuscript's real problem, and they are **mechanical, bounded, and — critically — they did not get fixed across two prior passes** (the flagged passages survive verbatim, which is a pipeline failure, not a judgment call). Three patterns dominate. First, **systemic uncontracted narration** (M1): Iris is a human POV who must contract per Rule 4 and her own voice card, yet "does not / it would not / I have not" recur across fifteen chapters, and two of them are outright typos ("Pipes don weep," "you don stop" — M8) that *should* be contractions and read as dropped letters. Second, the **"set it down / I want that plain" signature family** (M2) runs ~20+ against a 12 budget — the bible itself names this a cross-book model fingerprint to cap hard. Third, the **"the way X verbs Y" simile frame** (M3) is at/over its 12 budget; per §3.6's sibling-group rule, swapping to "like one does" won't help — these need to become direct verbs.

Secondary motif overages: "Settled/Kept on" bleeds out of Edith's EXCEPTION-protected ledger voice into Iris's and Marian's narration (M4 — the author is borrowing Edith's tic for the narrators, which collapses the deliberate two-hands device); "Lot [number]" at 26 vs 18 (M7); "the dent" at 11–13 vs 8 (M9); the steadying-count at 9 vs 6 and not tapering (M6, though ch23 executes the taper correctly — the overage is in the middle chapters). One slop-lexicon cluster in ch17–18 ("quiet/held/listening," m2). The good news: every one of these is a grep-and-trim operation with clear targets, and several "fixes" overlap (correcting the two typos in M8 also resolves two M1 instances). **This needs one disciplined mechanical pass, actually applied this time.**

### 4. Genre-Savvy Beta Reader

As a reader who lives on the literary-gothic shelf, I was genuinely held. The mundane-horror register is the draw: a house that counts lot numbers in the wall instead of whispering, a dial tone that answers half a second late, a culvert that washes out by ordinary weather and seals you in more completely than any locked door — this is *smart* horror, and the catalog-voice ("Condition: held") is a real pleasure. The two scenes that gutted me are the ones the book is built around: ch20 (the thing wearing Danny, and the unbearable detail that it knows she kept his red marble and watched her own reflection while he drowned — that's a genuinely devastating use of the lure) and ch23–24 (the burning that doesn't work, the crawl up the slope, the door left ajar that wants her to come in out of the killing cold). The realization in ch24 that "out" is death and "in" is being-kept, and that her own hypothermic body is lying to her with false warmth — that's the best beat in the book.

Where my interest flagged: the **back third repeats its own discoveries** (ch21 and ch22 telling me again what ch16 already made me feel), and **ch19 felt like I'd read it in ch11** — same window, same pond, same small shape, same reasoning-it-away, same refusal. By the second window-watch I was ahead of Iris, which a horror reader doesn't want. The **coda lost me slightly** because Marian sounds *exactly* like Iris — when the new archivist started counting to steady herself, the trap should have felt like a fresh person walking into the jaws, but instead it felt like Iris in a different coat, which softened the "oh no, it's happening again" punch. Tighten those and this is a book I'd press on people. The bones are excellent; it's the echoes that pull me out.

### 5. Adversarial Reviewer

Let me be brutal where brutality is earned, then credit what works. **The single most damning fact about this manuscript is that I can quote the prior review's flagged passages and find them in the current text, unchanged.** "Pipes don weep in the print of a foot" (ch9) — flagged, still there, and it's a *typo* in a book whose entire aesthetic is precision. "I want that on the record" / "I want that plain" recurs like a nervous tic across a dozen chapters against a hard budget the bible itself calls a model fingerprint. The "the way you [verb]" simile frame — "the way you touch the rungs of a ladder" (ch1), "the way the tide goes off a stone" (ch9), "the way a drowning hand takes a rope" (ch11) — sits at/over budget, and per the guide's own §3.6 the cure is NOT another simile, yet the manuscript keeps reaching for the frame. This is the **Steady B+ problem (§3.3) made visible**: the prose maintains a uniform, controlled, faintly over-written register, and the controlled-character interiority (Iris counts to steady herself; Iris measures to find truth) is *exactly* the cross-book fingerprint §3.2 warns about — the counter-measurer protagonist. The book half-earns it because counting is the premise — but then it gives the **same counting reflex to Marian in the coda** (§3.2's nightmare: protagonist after protagonist across books soothing by counting), which is indefensible and flattens the one place the book most needs a second voice.

Now: the weakest *pages*. Ch19 is the worst chapter because it's a Xerox of ch11 — window, pond, small shape, deer/body reasoning, count, refuse, leave — and a horror novella cannot afford to spend its penultimate-act real estate re-running an earlier beat. Ch21 and ch22 are guilty of the **telling-after-showing safety net (§3.2)** at the structural scale: ch16 *showed* Iris realizing she's the next entry, and ch21/22 *tell* it again twice more, each time re-walking the warm-room mechanism the reader has held since ch13. And the coda stages its reveal twice (ledger, then wall-count), the second restating the first.

What works — and it genuinely does: the **bureaucratic-horror concept is fresh and fully executed**; the **dial-tone-lag → Danny-thing-lag payoff** (ch2/10 → ch20) is real craft; ch20's lure is one of the better haunted-house confrontations I've read because it weaponizes guilt the reader watched the protagonist bury; and ch23–24's wrong-belief climax and grounded-hypothermia door (the warmth is in her dying body, not the house — §"never magic" honored) are exactly right. The architecture is sound; the reveals are deduced, not delivered; the trap closes legitimately. This is a good book wearing a layer of un-applied edits. Strip the un-landed voice pass, kill the ch19 re-run, stop re-explaining ch16's discovery, and give Marian her own throat — and the adversary has very little left to attack.
